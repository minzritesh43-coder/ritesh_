## 1. INTRODUCTION

**Life processes** = the basic functions performed by living organisms to maintain their life and to keep them alive.

**Criteria of life:**
- **Visible movement** is NOT a definite criterion (e.g., a tree, a sleeping animal appear still but are alive).
- **Molecular movement** inside the body is the real indicator of life.
- Growth, respiration, reproduction, response to stimuli are additional criteria.

**Why are life processes needed?**
Living structures are made of molecules that keep breaking down due to wear and tear. To repair and maintain them, energy is required. This energy comes from **food (nutrition)** and is released by **respiration**, transported by the **transportation system**, and waste produced is removed by **excretion**.

**Four main life processes:**
1. Nutrition
2. Respiration
3. Transportation
4. Excretion

> Unicellular organisms exchange materials directly with the environment by diffusion. Multicellular organisms have specialised tissues/organs because all cells are not in direct contact with the environment; diffusion alone is too slow and insufficient.

## 2. NUTRITION

**Nutrition:** The process of taking in food and utilising it for energy, growth, repair and maintenance.

**Why needed:** Source of energy + raw materials for building the body.

### 2.1 Modes of Nutrition

| Mode | Definition | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| **Autotrophic** | Organism makes its own food from simple inorganic substances (CO₂, H₂O) using sunlight | Green plants, blue-green algae (cyanobacteria) |
| **Heterotrophic** | Organism depends on other organisms for food | Animals, fungi, most bacteria |

**Types of Heterotrophic Nutrition:**
- **Saprophytic (Saprotrophic):** Feeds on dead and decaying organic matter by secreting digestive enzymes outside the body (extracellular digestion). → *Fungi (bread mould, Rhizopus, yeast), mushrooms, many bacteria.*
- **Parasitic:** Derives nutrition from a living host, harming it. → *Cuscuta (amarbel), ticks, lice, leeches, tapeworm, Plasmodium.*
- **Holozoic:** Takes in whole/complex food, then digests it internally. → *Amoeba, Paramecium, humans.*

---

### 2.2 AUTOTROPHIC NUTRITION — PHOTOSYNTHESIS

**Definition:** The process by which autotrophs take in CO₂ and water and convert them into carbohydrates in the presence of sunlight and chlorophyll.

**Equation:**
```
                 Sunlight
6CO₂ + 12H₂O  ─────────────►  C₆H₁₂O₆ + 6O₂ + 6H₂O
                Chlorophyll
```

**Raw materials:** CO₂, water, sunlight, chlorophyll.

**Main events of photosynthesis:**
1. **Absorption of light energy by chlorophyll.**
2. **Conversion of light energy into chemical energy** and **splitting (photolysis) of water molecules** into hydrogen and oxygen.
3. **Reduction of CO₂ to carbohydrates.**

> These steps need not occur one after the other immediately. E.g., in desert plants (CAM plants) stomata open at night, CO₂ is taken in and stored as an acid, which is used during the day.

**Site:** Chloroplast (contains chlorophyll) — mostly in mesophyll cells of leaves.
Chloroplast is called the **"kitchen of the cell."**

**Entry of raw materials:**
- **CO₂** → through **stomata** (tiny pores on leaf surface). Large amounts of gaseous exchange also occur across the surface of stems, roots and leaves.
- **Water** → absorbed from soil by roots → xylem → leaves.
- **Minerals** (N, P, Fe, Mg) → from soil as ions. **Nitrogen** is essential for proteins; taken as nitrates/nitrites or as organic nitrogen.

**Stomata:**
- Each stoma is bounded by two **guard cells**.
- **Opening:** Guard cells swell (turgid) when water flows in → pore opens.
- **Closing:** Guard cells shrink (flaccid) → pore closes.
- Stomata close mainly to **prevent excessive water loss by transpiration** when CO₂ is not required.

**Experiments to remember:**
- **Starch test (iodine test)** — proves starch is formed in photosynthesis. Destarch plant → keep in sunlight → boil leaf in water → boil in alcohol (to remove chlorophyll) → add iodine → **blue-black** colour shows starch.
- **Variegated leaf experiment** — proves chlorophyll is necessary (only green portions turn blue-black).
- **Experiment with KOH** (absorbs CO₂) — proves CO₂ is necessary.

### 2.3 HETEROTROPHIC NUTRITION EXAMPLES

**Amoeba (holozoic, unicellular):**
- Takes in food using temporary finger-like extensions — **pseudopodia**, which fuse over the food particle forming a **food vacuole**.
- Complex substances broken down inside the food vacuole by enzymes → diffuse into cytoplasm.
- Undigested material moved to the surface and thrown out (egestion).

**Paramecium:** Has a definite shape; food is taken in at a specific spot (**oral groove**) by the movement of **cilia**.

### 2.4 NUTRITION IN HUMAN BEINGS

**Alimentary canal:** Mouth → Oesophagus → Stomach → Small intestine → Large intestine → Rectum → Anus.
**Associated glands:** Salivary glands, Liver, Pancreas.

#### (a) Mouth / Buccal cavity
- **Teeth** — chew and grind food (physical digestion), increasing surface area.
- **Tongue** — mixes food with saliva, helps in swallowing, has taste buds.
- **Salivary glands** secrete **saliva** containing enzyme **salivary amylase (ptyalin)**.
  - Starch → Maltose (sugar). Medium: slightly acidic/neutral.
- Food becomes a soft slippery **bolus**.

#### (b) Oesophagus (food pipe)
- Food is pushed down by rhythmic contraction of muscles — **peristaltic movement** (peristalsis). Occurs all along the gut.

#### (c) Stomach
- Thick muscular walls; churns food.
- **Gastric glands** secrete **gastric juice**:
  1. **Hydrochloric acid (HCl)** — creates acidic medium for pepsin to act; kills bacteria/germs.
  2. **Pepsin** — digests **proteins → peptones/peptides**.
  3. **Mucus** — protects the inner lining of the stomach from the action of acid.
- Exit of food regulated by the **pyloric sphincter** muscle.
- Semi-digested food is now called **chyme**.

> *Acidity/ulcers:* Excess acid → burning sensation; antacids (bases) neutralise it. Ulcers are often caused by the bacterium *Helicobacter pylori*.

#### (d) Small Intestine
- **Longest part** of the alimentary canal (~6 m); fitted into a compact space by coiling.
- Length varies with diet: **herbivores have a longer small intestine** (cellulose digestion takes time); **carnivores have a shorter one** (meat is easier to digest).
- Site of **complete digestion** of carbohydrates, proteins and fats.
- Receives secretions from **liver** and **pancreas**.

**Bile juice (from liver, stored in gall bladder):**
- **Alkaline** — makes the acidic food from the stomach alkaline for pancreatic enzymes to act.
- **Emulsification of fats** — breaks large fat globules into smaller globules, increasing efficiency of enzyme action. (Bile has no enzymes.)

**Pancreatic juice (from pancreas):**
- **Trypsin** → digests proteins
- **Pancreatic amylase** → digests starch
- **Lipase** → breaks down emulsified fats

**Intestinal juice (from glands in the intestinal wall):**
- Completes digestion:
  - Carbohydrates → **Glucose**
  - Proteins → **Amino acids**
  - Fats → **Fatty acids + Glycerol**

**Absorption:**
- Inner lining of small intestine has finger-like projections — **villi**.
- Villi **increase surface area** for absorption; richly supplied with blood vessels.
- Absorbed food is carried by blood to every cell for **energy, building new tissues and repair**.

#### (e) Large Intestine
- Absorbs **water** (and some salts) from the unabsorbed food.
- Remaining waste is **egested** through the **anus**, regulated by the **anal sphincter**.

**Summary table of digestion:**

| Region | Secretion | Enzyme | Action |
|---|---|---|---|
| Mouth | Saliva | Salivary amylase | Starch → Maltose |
| Stomach | Gastric juice | Pepsin (+HCl, mucus) | Proteins → Peptones |
| Small intestine | Bile (liver) | – | Emulsifies fats, makes medium alkaline |
| Small intestine | Pancreatic juice | Trypsin, Amylase, Lipase | Proteins→amino acids; Starch→sugar; Fats→fatty acids+glycerol |
| Small intestine | Intestinal juice | Erepsin, maltase, lipase | Completes digestion |

## 3. RESPIRATION

**Respiration:** The process of breaking down food (glucose) to release energy, which is stored as **ATP**.

**Breathing vs Respiration:**
- *Breathing* — physical process of inhaling O₂ and exhaling CO₂.
- *Respiration* — biochemical process of oxidation of food inside cells to release energy.

### 3.1 Breakdown of Glucose

```
                    Glucose (6C)
                         │  (in cytoplasm — Glycolysis)
                         ▼
                 Pyruvate (3C) + Energy
      ┌──────────────────┼────────────────────────┐
  (No oxygen)        (Lack of O₂,           (Presence of O₂)
   in yeast          in our muscles)          in mitochondria
      ▼                   ▼                        ▼
Ethanol + CO₂        Lactic acid            CO₂ + H₂O + Energy
 + Energy             + Energy               (much more energy)
(Anaerobic/          (Anaerobic)              (Aerobic)
 Fermentation)
```

- **Glycolysis** occurs in the **cytoplasm**; aerobic respiration occurs in the **mitochondria**.
- **Mitochondria = "powerhouse of the cell."**
- **Aerobic respiration releases much more energy than anaerobic respiration.**
- **Muscle cramps** occur during sudden vigorous activity due to accumulation of **lactic acid** (anaerobic respiration in muscles due to lack of oxygen).

**ATP (Adenosine Triphosphate):** The **energy currency** of the cell.
- ADP + Pi + energy → ATP
- Breakdown of one ATP molecule releases **30.5 kJ/mol** of energy.
- ATP is used for muscle contraction, protein synthesis, nerve impulse conduction, etc.

### 3.2 Respiration in Plants
- Exchange of gases through **stomata** (leaves) and **lenticels** (stems), and through root surfaces.
- Direction of gas exchange depends on need & time:
  - **Night:** only respiration → CO₂ released.
  - **Day:** photosynthesis dominates → net O₂ released; CO₂ produced in respiration is used up.

### 3.3 Respiration in Aquatic vs Terrestrial Organisms
- Water contains **dissolved oxygen** which is **far less** than the amount of O₂ in air.
- Hence aquatic animals **breathe faster** and use **gills** (large surface area, richly supplied with blood vessels).
- Terrestrial organisms use atmospheric O₂ — lungs, tracheae (insects), skin (earthworm), etc.

### 3.4 Human Respiratory System

**Path of air:**
Nostrils → Nasal cavity → Pharynx → Larynx → Trachea (windpipe) → Bronchi → Bronchioles → Alveoli

- **Nostrils:** Hair and mucus filter dust; air is warmed and moistened.
- **Trachea:** Supported by **rings of cartilage** so that it does not collapse when there is less air.
- **Alveoli:** balloon-like structures — **site of gas exchange**.
  - Provide a very **large surface area** (~80 m² in humans).
  - Walls are **thin (one cell thick)** and contain an extensive network of **blood capillaries**.
  - O₂ diffuses into blood; CO₂ diffuses out.

**Mechanism of breathing:**
- **Inhalation:** Diaphragm flattens (moves down), ribs lift up and outward → chest cavity volume increases → air rushes in.
- **Exhalation:** Diaphragm moves up (domes), ribs move down and inward → volume decreases → air pushed out.
- **Residual volume:** Lungs always contain a residual volume of air so that there is sufficient time for oxygen to be absorbed and CO₂ to be released.

**Transport of gases:**
- **Oxygen** is carried by the respiratory pigment **haemoglobin** (in RBCs), which has a high affinity for oxygen. Its deficiency causes **anaemia**.
- **CO₂** is more soluble in water, so it is mostly transported in **dissolved form in the plasma**.

> **Rate of breathing:** ~15–18 times/minute in an adult at rest.

## 4. TRANSPORTATION

### 4.1 TRANSPORTATION IN HUMAN BEINGS

**Blood** = a **fluid connective tissue**.
Components: **Plasma** (fluid medium) + **RBCs** + **WBCs** + **Platelets**.
- Plasma transports food, CO₂, nitrogenous wastes in dissolved form.
- RBCs carry oxygen (haemoglobin).
- Platelets help in **clotting** of blood at the site of injury, preventing loss of blood.

#### The Human Heart
- A **muscular organ**, about the size of our fist.
- Has **four chambers**: Right atrium, Right ventricle, Left atrium, Left ventricle.
- **Why four chambers?** To prevent the mixing of oxygenated and deoxygenated blood — this allows a **highly efficient supply of oxygen**, needed by warm-blooded animals (birds & mammals) to maintain body temperature.

**Flow of blood (Double circulation):**
1. **Deoxygenated** blood from body → **Vena cava** → **Right atrium** → (contracts) → **Right ventricle** → **Pulmonary artery** → **Lungs** (oxygenated)
2. **Oxygenated** blood → **Pulmonary vein** → **Left atrium** → (contracts) → **Left ventricle** → **Aorta** → **Whole body**

- **Valves** ensure that blood flows only in one direction (prevent backflow).
- **Left ventricle has the thickest walls** because it pumps blood to the entire body at high pressure.
- When atria contract, ventricles are relaxed, and vice versa.

**Double Circulation:** Blood passes through the heart **twice** in one complete cycle of the body.
- **Pulmonary circulation:** Heart → Lungs → Heart
- **Systemic circulation:** Heart → Body → Heart

> **Note:** Fishes have a **two-chambered heart**; blood goes only once through the heart (single circulation). Amphibians and many reptiles have **three-chambered hearts** and tolerate some mixing of blood since they do not regulate body temperature.

#### Blood Vessels

| Feature | Arteries | Veins | Capillaries |
|---|---|---|---|
| Direction | Carry blood **away** from heart | Carry blood **to** the heart | Connect arteries & veins |
| Blood | Oxygenated (except pulmonary artery) | Deoxygenated (except pulmonary vein) | – |
| Walls | Thick, elastic (high pressure) | Thin | **One-cell thick** |
| Valves | Absent | **Present** (prevent backflow) | Absent |

**Blood Pressure:**
- **Systolic pressure:** pressure in artery during ventricular contraction — normal **120 mm Hg**.
- **Diastolic pressure:** pressure during ventricular relaxation — normal **80 mm Hg**.
- Measured by a **sphygmomanometer**.
- **Hypertension (high BP):** 150/90 mm Hg or more — caused by narrowing of arterioles; can lead to rupture of arteries and internal bleeding.

#### Lymph (Tissue fluid)
- Formed when some plasma, proteins and blood cells escape through the pores of capillaries into intercellular spaces.
- Colourless, contains less protein than blood.
- **Functions:** Carries digested and **absorbed fats from the intestine**; drains **excess fluid** from extracellular space back into the blood; part of the immune system.

### 4.2 TRANSPORTATION IN PLANTS

Plants have low energy needs and use a slow transport system. Two conducting tissues:

#### (a) XYLEM — transport of water and minerals
- Made of **tracheids and vessels** (dead cells) — form continuous tubes from root to leaf.
- **Mechanism:**
  1. **Root pressure:** Ions are actively taken up by root cells → increases ion concentration → water moves in from soil by **osmosis** → creates a column of water pushed upward. (Significant at night.)
  2. **Transpiration pull:** Evaporation of water from stomata creates a suction that pulls water up from the xylem. This is the **main driving force in daytime**, especially in tall plants.

**Transpiration:** Loss of water in the form of vapour from the aerial parts of a plant.
**Functions of transpiration:**
- Helps in the absorption and upward movement of water and minerals.
- Helps in **temperature regulation** (cooling) of the plant.

#### (b) PHLOEM — translocation of food
- **Translocation:** Transport of soluble products of photosynthesis (mainly **sucrose**), amino acids and other substances.
- Made of **sieve tubes and companion cells** (living).
- Transport is **bidirectional** — from leaves to storage organs (roots, fruits, seeds) and from storage organs to growing parts.
- **Mechanism:** Material is transferred into sieve tubes using **energy from ATP**; this increases osmotic pressure → water enters → pressure moves the material to tissues of lower pressure.

**Xylem vs Phloem:**

| Xylem | Phloem |
|---|---|
| Transports water & minerals | Transports food (sucrose, amino acids) |
| Unidirectional (upward) | Bidirectional |
| Dead cells (mostly) | Living cells |
| Uses physical forces (transpiration pull) — no energy | Requires energy (ATP) |

## 5. EXCRETION

**Excretion:** The biological process of removing harmful **nitrogenous metabolic wastes** from the body.

### 5.1 Human Excretory System
**Organs:** A pair of **kidneys**, a pair of **ureters**, a **urinary bladder**, and a **urethra**.

- **Kidneys:** Located in the abdomen, one on either side of the backbone. Filter blood and form urine.
- **Ureters:** Carry urine from kidneys to urinary bladder.
- **Urinary bladder:** Stores urine (muscular, under nervous control).
- **Urethra:** Urine passes out through it.

**Main excretory product:** **Urea** (formed in the **liver** from the breakdown of excess amino acids). Others: uric acid, creatinine, excess water, salts.

#### The Nephron
- **Nephron = basic filtration unit of the kidney.** Each kidney has about **one million nephrons**.

**Parts & Steps:**
1. **Glomerulus** — a cluster of thin-walled capillaries inside **Bowman's capsule**. Blood enters through the **renal artery** under high pressure → **ultrafiltration**: water, glucose, amino acids, salts, urea pass into the Bowman's capsule. Blood cells and proteins are retained.
2. **Tubular part (selective reabsorption)** — as the filtrate flows along the tubule, useful substances such as **glucose, amino acids, salts and most water** are **selectively reabsorbed** into the blood capillaries surrounding the tubule.
3. **Collecting duct** — collects urine from many nephrons → carried to ureter.

**Amount of water reabsorbed depends on:**
- How much **excess water** there is in the body.
- How much **dissolved waste** needs to be excreted.

> Humans produce about **1–1.8 litres of urine per day**.

#### Artificial Kidney (Haemodialysis)
- Used when kidneys fail (kidney failure → toxins accumulate → death).
- A machine containing tubes made of **selectively permeable membrane** placed in a tank of **dialysing fluid** (has the same osmotic pressure as blood but **no nitrogenous wastes**).
- Waste products diffuse out from the blood into the dialysing fluid; purified blood is returned to the body.
- **Difference from kidney:** There is **no reabsorption** in an artificial kidney.

### 5.2 Excretion in Plants
Plants lack a special excretory organ. They use several strategies:
- **Oxygen and CO₂** — released through **stomata and lenticels**.
- **Excess water** — removed by **transpiration**.
- Waste products stored in **cell vacuoles**, or as **resins and gums** especially in **old xylem**.
- Some wastes stored in **leaves that fall off**.
- Some wastes excreted into the **soil** around them.
